All figures: residual = station minus ERA5 (°C); positive values mean ERA5 is cooler. Main screened sample: 6,958,414 observations, 4,217 stations; 2020-01-01 to 2025-08-31. Each panel uses finite values for its own covariates. Means weight observations equally. Intervals are pointwise 95% percentile intervals from 1,000 station bootstrap resamples within each bin, retaining all observations from sampled stations. They do not account for dependence between different stations. The main sample retains mixed observation-day windows; it is not restricted to the UTC subset. NDVI includes 1,873,268 observations with explicitly flagged carry-forward from the latest available matching season.

01_covariates: Binned mean residuals, with quantile bins and shaded confidence intervals. Points use bin midpoints, bounded by observed extrema; connecting lines guide the eye. These are descriptive conditional means, not model-based partial dependence. The precipitation proxy uses log10(max(tp, 1e-9)) in metres; it is not a daily total. Wind is the magnitude of the daily mean vector. Elevation excludes 3,825 missing, nonfinite or sentinel-coded observations. Covariates refer to the observation-source location. Duplicate quantile boundaries are merged.

02_urbanization: Observation-weighted mean residuals and station-bootstrap confidence intervals by land urbanization class. Water is excluded. Numbers give distinct stations.

03_interactions: Mean residuals in joint quantile bins, drawn at their numeric boundaries. Both panels share a symmetric colour scale centred on zero. Grey cells have fewer than 10 stations or no data. Elevation excludes −9999 and −999. The panels describe associations, not causal interactions.

04_station_map: Mean residual at each station. European window: 4,217 of 4,217 stations, 25°W–45°E and 34–72°N. Stations outside the window are omitted from this map only. Lambert conformal projection; Natural Earth 1:50m land, coastline and national boundaries. Colours saturate beyond ±3°C; the endpoint labels indicate saturation.
